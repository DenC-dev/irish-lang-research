How far does typed Irish sit from the written standard?

Síneadh is a paper and a small set of open rules for measuring that distance in Irish learned at school and Irish written online. Read the paper or get the code.

Edited proseBhí mo chara ag ár dteach inné.
What the rules noticeBhi mo cara ag ár teach inne.
  • Bhi and inne: the length mark is missing (bhí, inné).
  • cara after mo: the standard writes lenition (chara).
  • teach after ár: the standard writes eclipsis (dteach).

Both sentences are made up for this page: "My friend was at our house yesterday." The rules compare text with the written standard, so they also flag some forms that are normal in a dialect. They are not for grading anyone's Irish.

The two mutation rows share one scale. Every other pair is scaled to its own larger value. The tweet figure for lenition could plausibly lie anywhere from 7.0% to 12.9%. An earlier version of this page reported a larger eclipsis gap from a narrower rule; the wider check did not support it.

Tweets moved toward the standard over the 2010s

The tweet collection is two samples, one earlier and one later. In the later one, missing length marks fall by two thirds and English falls too. Emoji is the one line going up. Unwritten lenition also drops, from 12.2% to 7.0%, though at these counts that could be chance.

  • Phones gained Irish keyboards and predictive text over these years, which would produce this pattern.
  • So would more institutions and professionals tweeting in Irish.
  • The samples overlap in time and were drawn in different ways, so this is a lead to follow up.

None of this says who wrote the tweets

No public collection of online Irish records whether a writer grew up with the language, learned it in an Irish-medium school, or picked it up as an adult. A noun written without its mutation can come from any of these:

  • Typing fast
  • Autocorrect
  • An English noun
  • A dialect pattern
  • Writing informally
  • Still learning the rule

There is also no single "Gaelscoil Irish" to detect. In the main study of pupils' speech, the share of utterances with an error ran from 20.5% in one school to 41.6% in another, and the Irish of young Gaeltacht speakers is changing as well.

Five questions nobody has answered yet

Ordered by how much is missing against how doable the work is. The repository lists eighteen.

  1. Do language models get wrong what learners get wrong?

    Models and fluent speakers fail on different parts of Irish grammar. Nobody has set the models' pattern beside the learners' pattern.

    Needs no new data from people.

  2. What Irish are the models trained on?

    Most of the Irish Wikipedia was reportedly written by two editors without fluent Irish, and it feeds Irish language models.

    Needs fluent readers to rate a sample of the sources.

  3. Who is writing?

    Online collections hold text and nothing about the writer.

    Needs donated writing with a few background questions.

  4. What about sound?

    The best-documented features of new speakers' Irish are in pronunciation. Every computational result so far is about spelling and vocabulary.

    Needs measurement of recordings that speech projects already hold, with consent.

  5. What are pupils and teachers doing with generative AI?

    A government advisory paper warns of a divide for Irish-medium schools. No study has watched what happens in class.

    Needs ethics approval and partner schools.

Before you cite this

  • This is version 0.4, dated 16 November 2026. It has not been peer reviewed.
  • The author speaks Irish. A second speaker has not yet checked the examples or the word lists.
  • The literature search, the code and the drafting were done with an AI assistant under the author's direction.
  • The tweets are from one platform and stop in 2019. No open collection of later Irish tweets was found. The repository has a tool for gathering later posts through official access, and it has not been run yet.
  • The lenition result rests on 37 cases.
  • Nobody's age or background is recorded in the data, so nothing here is a claim about who wrote what.
  • No new data was collected from people and no platform was scraped. Only totals are published. Risk register

Run it yourself

Python 3.9 or later, nothing to install. The two text collections download from their own repositories under their own licences.

git clone https://github.com/DenC-dev/irish-lang-research
cd irish-lang-research
./scripts/fetch_ud.sh
python3 -I scripts/compare_treebanks.py --idt external/UD_Irish-IDT \
    --twittirish external/UD_Irish-TwittIrish --out results
python3 -I scripts/mutation_contexts.py --idt external/UD_Irish-IDT \
    --twittirish external/UD_Irish-TwittIrish --out results
python3 -I scripts/compare_cohorts.py --twittirish external/UD_Irish-TwittIrish --out results
python3 -I scripts/clause_density.py --idt external/UD_Irish-IDT \
    --twittirish external/UD_Irish-TwittIrish --out results
